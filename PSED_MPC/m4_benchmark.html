ALD dose optimization — characterizing executor strategies

An executable, ontology-linked replica of the Yanguas-Gil 2026 tool (yanguas_gil_saturation), used to fairly characterize two reproducible non-LLM executor strategies — an adaptive deterministic search and a classical grid — on identical targets, stopping criteria, noise, and CVD sweeps. This is not a re-run of their LLM agent, and the benchmark processes are not in our KB — see caveats.

1 · The tool is a faithful replica (reproduces their Fig. 3)

Dots = where the deterministic optimizer stops (target 98% of the saturated GPC). All 5 self-limited processes are optimized and the injected CVD variant is flagged — but read the panels below before reading that as a "win".

2 · Deterministic vs a classical grid baseline (fair non-LLM comparison)

Both are reproducible (σ=0) and hit similar ε (both target 98%). The honest contrast is efficiency: the adaptive search spends fewer tool samples by locating the saturation scale, while the grid spends a fixed budget over the whole dose range without needing a starting guess. Neither is an LLM; this is executor-vs-executor.

3 · ε is a stopping choice, not accuracy

Moving the saturation target trades total dose time for ε. The "1.5–2.6%" from the first draft was just the point at target=0.98; it is not an intrinsic precision.

4 · CVD detection is fragile (both strategies)

Solid = deterministic, dashed = grid. A strong CVD term (gr0≈0.05) is easy to flag; a weak one (gr0≲0.01) hides under the plateau tolerance for both executors, and noise erodes detection further. The earlier "flags CVD ✓" held only for the easy, noise-free case.

5 · Deterministic vs a stochastic search on the SAME tool

Grey = an illustrative sampling search (30 seeds) — not the LLM agent — included only to measure the run-to-run spread any stochastic method incurs here. The red line is the paper's reported o3 median ε, shown for scale, not as a controlled comparison.

6 · The real KB link for this task (and its limit)

The KB still has no GPC-vs-dose data, so the synthetic benchmark is not KB-grounded. But the honest link is now a physics-derived rate prior from a real, per-species initial sticking probability — curated from a 5th paper, Arts 2019 "Sticking probabilities of H₂O and Al(CH₃)₃" (TMA s₀=1.2×10⁻³, H₂O s₀ T-dependent), into the ontology species individuals: k1 = s₀·Φ/q, Φ = pA/√(2πmkT). For Al₂O₃/TMA: s₀=0.0012 (species_sticking(arts2019)), q=5.0e+18 m⁻², pA=100.0 Pa → k1 ≈ 333 s⁻¹ (t_sat ≈ 9.0e-03 s). That is the flat-surface saturation time; the literature's ~0.1 s pulses fill high-AR features (a transport problem — the M3 twin's regime). So the KB now grounds the rate for a real chemistry per reactant, but not the synthetic benchmark's dose regime.

Caveats & scope (what this does and does not show)

  • Not a re-run of the agent. o3/GPT-5 numbers are quoted from Yanguas-Gil 2026, not measured here. A true head-to-head needs their agent on this exact tool.
  • Benchmark is synthetic + not in the KB. The 5 processes are the paper's Table I; the KB holds conformality data, not dose–GPC data, so "KB-primed" does not apply to them. The only real KB link is the rate bridge in panel 6 (Al₂O₃/TMA).
  • A bespoke optimizer vs a general reasoning agent is not a like-for-like test. Any classical search (deterministic OR grid, panel 2) beats an LLM on this narrow task; the paper's aim was to probe LLM reasoning, not optimizer quality. The defensible role here is as a reproducible executor the agent could call, not a competitor.
  • ε is tunable (panel 3) and CVD detection is fragile for both executors (panel 4) — shown explicitly rather than reported as single favorable numbers.
  • Determinism (σ=0) is a property of the method class (both non-LLM executors have it), not an earned advantage; panel 5 measures what a stochastic search actually costs on this tool.

  • Classical baseline. Done: a grid search is characterized alongside the deterministic executor (panels 2 & 4) under the same targets/noise/CVD sweep. Remaining: add Bayesian optimization for a sample-efficiency ceiling.
  • Score on real chemistries where the rate bridge (panel 5) sets the dose scale, and measure the sample reduction from the KB prior directly.
