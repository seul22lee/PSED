In this work, we investigated an approach of hot-wire assisted ALD (HWALD), utilizing a hot (up to 2000 8 C) tungsten (W) wire. Tungsten /uniFB01 lms were deposited by this method using alternating pulses of WF6 gas and atomic hydrogen (at-H). The latter was generated by catalytic dissociation of molecular hydrogen (H2) upon the hot-wire. The W /uniFB01 lms were grown on a 100-nm thick thermal SiO2. The growth process was monitored in real time by an in-situ spectroscopic ellipsometer (SE). The real-time SE monitoring revealed the coexistence of three processes: CVD, etching, and ALD of the W /uniFB01 lm. WF6 could back-stream diffuse to the hot-wire, resulting in WF6 decomposition and generation of a /uniFB02 ux of /uniFB02 uorine (F). The latter caused etching of the grown W /uniFB01 lm and the /uniFB01 lament, and provided extra tungsten supply, which might cause CVD. Higher pressure and higher carrier gas /uniFB02 ow rate were found to largely suppress the back-stream diffusion of WF6, which ef /uniFB01 ciently limited CVD. By controlling the dose of WF6 and process pressure, the etching had also been minimized. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy of optimized HWALD grown W revealed 99at% of W; concentrations of oxygen and /uniFB02 uorine were lower than 1%, below the detection limit.

2.3 Sample preparation In this experiment, tungsten /uniFB01 lms were deposited on a 100-nm-thick silicon oxide (SiO2) thermally grown on p-type Si (100) substrate. Standard substrate cleaning included 10 min dipping in a 99%HNO3 (room temperature), rinsing, and 10 min dipping in a 69% HNO3 (95 8 C). Finally, the substrate was immersed into a 0.3% HF solution for 3 min. As the adhesion is very poor between tungsten and SiO2 or Si3N4 [19], an adhesion layer is required [20]. In this work, a thin ( &lt; 10 nm) layer of amorphous silicon (a-Si) was /uniFB01 rst deposited on SiO2 by chemical vapour deposition (CVD) using disilane (Si2H6), at 325 8 C and a pressure of 10 mbar. The thickness of a-Si was in-situ monitored by SE. Next, the reactor was pumped down to a pressure of 1 mbar followed by introducing WF6 to convert the a-Si into W. Further, the reactor was /uniFB02 ushed by N2 for 10 min and evacuated back to 10 /C0 7 mbar, followed by the wafer transfer to the HWALD reactor 1, to continue with the ALD process. The HWALD of W /uniFB01 lms was carried out using sequential pulses of WF6 and at-H at substrate temperatures ranging between 200 and 325 8 C, and process pressures of 0.001 -1 mbar. For ex-situ (electrical) characterization, it is important to prevent oxidation of the grown W /uniFB01 lm during its exposure to air. The wafer was therefore transferred back to reactor 2, where a 5 -10 nm capping layer of a-Si was deposited (same deposition method as used for a-Si seed layer) on top of W, completing the process.

## 3 Results and discussion

3.1 The existence of CVD and etching modes From the literature, thermal dissociation of WF6 (in gas phase) starts at 750 8 C[27, 28]. Although the gas ring was installed at a distance of 70 cm below the hot-wire, the operating conditions (pressure, gas /uniFB02 ows) could greatly in /uniFB02 uence the upward diffusion of WF6 to the hot-wire. As a consequence, this WF6 will dissociate at the hot-wire, likely generating, among others, atomic /uniFB02 uorine (at-F). As the dissociation of WF6 occurs in equilibrium, any increase of concentration of WF6 will enhance the generation of at-F. It is reported that the lifetime of at-F can be quite long [29]. Additionally, both WF6 and /uniFB02 uorine can adsorb on the (cold) reactor walls. This provides a background supply of WF6and /uniFB02 uorine to the gas phase over the deposition cycles. Fluorine, generated from WF6 dissociation upon the hot-wire, can contribute to etching the hot-wire and regeneration of volatile tungsten precursor(s) (likely tungsten /uniFB02 uorides). 4 Summary and conclusion In this work, we studied a hot-wire assisted deposition of W /uniFB01 lms by sequential pulses of WF6 and at-H precursors in a cold-wall reactor. We demonstrated the coexistence of CVD, etching, and ALD modes and investigated the in /uniFB02 uence of process parameters on each mode. This allowed to independently enhance a selected mode and supress the others. A higher gas pressure strengthened etching whereas a lower pressure could enhance CVD. By selecting proper process pressure, substrate and hot-wire temperature, precursor-pulse, and purge times, optimal conditions were found to maintain the ALD mode. Under these chosen conditions, HWALD W /uniFB01 lms were deposited with W purity approaching 99 at%.
