## Which decisions must stay figure-specific

These cannot be encoded as a universal rule, and each is decided by evidence
this paper states explicitly:

1. **Spatial x axis ⇒ curve-level.** True for Figs. 4, 6, 7 — but Fig. 4 is a
   *calculation* over the same axis. The axis settles curve-vs-point granularity;
   it says nothing about measured-vs-modelled.
2. **Enumerated condition axis ⇒ separate runs.** False for Fig. 4 (H = 2, 1,
   0.5, 0.2 µm are model inputs). A condition axis only implies separate
   depositions when the paper says something was *deposited* at each setting.
3. **Two curves in one panel ⇒ two experiments.** False for Figs. 6 and 7, where
   the pair is one measurement plus its fit. Resolved by the caption's
   circles/line legend, not by curve count.
4. **Series label is a model name.** Fig. 5's axis is model identity. Any rule
   that treats a series axis as a process condition would fabricate three
   experiments here.
5. **Figure-level `source` propagates to series.** False for Figs. 6 and 7: the
   figure is `measured`, one of its two series is calculated. Source must be
   resolvable at series level when the caption distinguishes the curves.
