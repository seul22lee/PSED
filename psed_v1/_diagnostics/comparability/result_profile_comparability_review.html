Runtime admission coverage

A null canonical axis is canonicalization abstaining about the representation, not an absence of science. The previous loader dropped every such curve, which meant the one thing a comparability layer most needs to be able to say — “I know what this measures but not what it was normalized against” — could never be said, because those curves were not in the universe at all. Ambiguity was zero because ambiguity had been excluded.

Admission is now axis-by-axis. Of 498 scientific series, 406 are admitted (191 fully resolved, 215 partially resolved) and 92 are excluded — every one with an explicit reason (INSUFFICIENT_AXIS_SEMANTICS: the y axis carries a raw printed string such as “QCM frequency change (Hz)” and no ontology quantity). Profile series went 70 → 151, and the pair universe 2031 → 5339 (cross-paper 699 → 1066). Unexplained scientific drops: 0.

Genuine normalization ambiguity

19 curves in 10.1039_d0cp03358h carry axis_kind = normalized_thickness_of_unknown_denominator: the paper prints “Normalized thickness (-)”, the measurand resolves to normalized_thickness, and the basis is recorded as unknown. Against a curve whose normalization_definition is explicitly t_over_t_max, the runtime now answers AMBIGUOUS — not DIRECT, and not absence. Unknown representation is not unknown quantity.

Shape-only is opt-in. 153 pairs would qualify if a caller explicitly asks for a shape comparison; by default they are ambiguous, because turning “we cannot establish the scale” into a weaker claim the caller never made is its own kind of overclaim. Eligibility is deterministic: same measurand, both normalized, x axes already compatible, and the bases the only unresolved thing.

Canonical representation → runtime

The runtime consumes the canonical record; it does not re-read the printed label. For each axis it takes quantity, comparison_group, normalization_definition, any persisted projections, and the context attached to the transformations that were actually applied. Label inference survives only where the canonical layer said nothing, and is marked inferred so the two are never confused.

The regression this closed. 10.1039_d0cp03358h Fig. 9b prints its abscissa as “Dimensionless distance x̃”. The canonical layer had already resolved it to dimensionless_distance normalized by x_over_feature_height, found feature_height = 0.1 µm from the series label “100 nm”, applied denormalize_x_by_feature_height with status converted, and persisted the projection to spatial_coordinate. The previous runtime saw only the label, found no basis in it, and reported NORMALIZATION_UNKNOWN and missing_context — discarding a resolved answer and then guessing at it. It now reads NORMALIZATION_EXPLICIT and reuses the resolved parameter, with provenance pointing at the canonical record rather than a weaker copy.

Corpus-wide the correction removed 153 false ambiguous verdicts (every one of them), promoted 153 pairs to DIRECT_PROFILE, and made 782 pairs execute through CANONICAL_PROJECTION rather than recomputing. Unexplained canonical/runtime disagreements: 0.

The 828 missing_context pairs did not move, and that is the correct outcome: 782 are blocked on cycle_number for the growth-per-cycle ↔ thickness bridge, and 46 additionally on a feature_height the canonical layer itself recorded as ambiguous for those curves. Canonical ambiguity stays ambiguity.

Why a canonical quantity id is not enough

t/t_entrance, t/t_max and t/t_planar all canonicalise to normalized_thickness with unit 1, and they are three different physical statements — how much thinner the film got relative to the mouth of the feature, relative to wherever this profile happened to peak, or relative to a flat witness coupon. So the comparison identity is (quantity, comparison group, normalization definition). Two normalized profiles with different bases come back RELATED_NOT_COMPARABLE; a normalized profile whose basis nobody recorded comes back ambiguous, which is a different answer from “no” and a very different answer from “yes”.

What this does and does not claim

The status answers a narrow question: whether two results can be placed in one representation, and what it costs to get them there. It does not claim the two experiments should agree, that their process conditions were equivalent, or that a SHAPE_ONLY pair is quantitatively equal. Transformations come only from declared ontology relations and extracted context — no curve is scaled, shifted or fitted to improve agreement, and a parameter nobody extracted stays missing rather than being filled from a typical literature value.
