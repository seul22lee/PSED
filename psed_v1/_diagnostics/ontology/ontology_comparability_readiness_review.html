The readiness contract largely already exists. quantity_relations carries 24 comparison groups each with a canonical unit, 11 normalization definitions with explicit numerator/denominator roles, 25 typed transformation rules, and — the part that matters most for an engine — 9 transformation statuses that already separate a transform which ran from one whose context is missing_context or ambiguous. That is the operational/semantic distinction a comparability engine needs, and it was designed in rather than being something this audit had to invent. Every reference in that structure resolves: 0 dangling across groups, normalizations, transforms, same_as, rule types and specializes.

Quantity vs representation vs normalization vs transform

The ontology draws the line at the quantity, and encodes representation by giving the normalized form its own id: film_thickness and normalized_thickness are separate quantities, related by a declared transform whose bridge is reference_thickness. The reference is then pinned not on the quantity but in normalization_definitions, which is what makes “normalized” answerable rather than vague: t_over_t_entrance, t_over_t_max and t_over_t_planar are three different physical statements that would otherwise share one label. The same applies on the abscissa: spatial_coordinate and dimensionless_distance are distinct, with five separate definitions for what the length was divided by (feature height, channel length, feature depth, hydraulic diameter, feature width).

So the four layers are represented — measurand as the quantity, representation as a sibling quantity, reference as a normalization definition, transform as a typed rule — but the representation layer is carried in the quantity id rather than as metadata on a shared measurand. That is a design choice with a real consequence, recorded under architecture decisions below.

Architecture decisions

ONTOLOGY_REPRESENTATION_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION. Representation is encoded in the quantity id (normalized_thickness, dimensionless_distance) rather than as metadata on a shared measurand. This works today and the normalization definitions keep the reference explicit. The cost appears when an engine must ask “is this the same physical quantity as that”: it has to consult the transform table rather than read a field, and a profile normalized by an unknown reference has no id distinct from one normalized by a known reference. Options: (a) keep as is and let the engine treat the transform table as authoritative; (b) add a `representation` + `reference` pair on the record, leaving ids unchanged; (c) migrate to measurand + representation metadata. Corpus impact of (c) is large — normalized_thickness alone appears in persisted results — so no partial migration was attempted. Recommended: (b), decided together with the engine.

ONTOLOGY_RATIO_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION. A ratio of two quantities of the same kind has no numerator/denominator species roles in the schema. normalization_definitions solves this for thickness and position by naming a denominator role, but a gas flow ratio would need the numerator and denominator to each carry a reagent, which the qualifier model (one by: reactant per quantity) cannot express. This is why flow_ratio is not added here: a weak dimensionless quantity with no way to say which gases it relates would record less than the current honest refusal.

Deferred

flow_ratio (blocked on the ratio decision above); electrode_potential and critical_angle (no corpus canonical usage — revisit when a record needs them); the role-prefixed composites (precursor_pulse_time, coreactant_purge_time and siblings) which are pipeline-emitted and heavily used but have no ontology entry; 147 defined quantities with no current corpus support, which a broader ontology may legitimately carry.
