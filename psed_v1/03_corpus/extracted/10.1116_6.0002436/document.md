## ABSTRACT

This work studied the growth of molybdenum oxide thin films with thermal atomic layer deposition (ALD) using Mo( i PrCp)2H2 and O3 as precursors. Growth parameters were determined by varying growth temperature and precursor dose times. ALD growth was exhibited in a temperature range of 100 -200 °C. The growth per cycle ranged from 1.3 to 1.7 Å/cycle with a standard uniformity parameter of &lt;5%. Attempts to grow films using H2O as an oxygen source showed no significant growth. Film properties were measured using spectroscopic ellipsometry, x-ray reflectivity, x-ray diffraction, x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, and infrared spectroscopy.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Atomic layer deposition (ALD) is a widely used subset of chemical vapor deposition that yields conformal films with precise thickness control. 1 Through alternating vapor phase precursor exposures, a series of self-limiting reactions take place on the surface of a substrate resulting in film growth. Films deposited using ALD can be grown on large substrate areas while maintaining high uniformity and conformality. 1 Because of these unique properties, ALD has been used to deposit a wide range of materials for semiconductors and other device and material applications. 2

Within ALD materials, ultrathin transition metal oxides have seen a particularly wide interest and use ranging from dielectrics to semiconducting oxides. The compositional focus of this work, MoOx (x = ∼ 3), has attracted interest for its electrical and catalytic properties. 3,4 MoOx also produced promising results as a holeselective material for application in solar cells such as in silicon heterojunctions. 5 -7 MoO3 has previously been grown through various methods including ALD, thermal evaporation, sputtering, solution casting, and CVD. 8 -12 Notable precursors for ALD of MoO3 include molybdenum hexacarbonyl (Mo(CO)6) 13 and Bis (t-butylimido)bis(dimethylamino)molybdenum(VI)

[( t BuN)2(NMe2)2Mo]. 6 Mo(CO)6 and ozone (O3) have been used to grow substoichiometric molybdenum oxide (x &lt; 3) thin films in a temperature range of 152 -172 °C. A growth rate of 0.7 Å/cycle was found at a growth temperature of 167 °C. The low thermal stability of Mo(CO)6 is shown by the narrow 10 °C thermal growth window leading to nonuniform growth and CVD-like behavior across a wide range of temperatures. ( t BuN)2(NMe2)2Mo and O3 have been reported to grow high purity MoOx films at deposition temperatures of 100 -300 °C, but with a low growth rate of 0.4 Å/cycle. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) on these films demonstrated low levels of carbon contamination. However, the somewhat narrow ALD temperature window of Mo(CO)6 and the low growth per cycle (GPC) of the amido-based Mo precursor motivate the investigation of new Mo precursors that will expand the temperature range and growth window.

In this work, bis-isopropyl cyclopentadienyl molybdenum dihydride [Mo( i PrCp)2H2] was investigated as a molybdenum precursor with O3 and H2O as oxygen precursors. The ALD temperature window and growth behavior was quantified along with the physical and chemical properties of the films. Films grown using this precursor were compared to films grown using ( t BuN)2(NMe2)2Mo and attempts to grow films using Mo(CO)6 are also discussed.

ALD using Mo( i PrCp)2H2 (Ereztech, LLC) and O3 or H2O was investigated by adjusting the Mo precursor temperature, deposition temperature, precursor pulse time, and oxidant pulse time to determine suitable conditions for growth and the ALD temperature window. O3 was pulsed with a concentration of ∼ 3 wt. % in O2 through an orifice into the ALD chamber. Mo precursor and oxidant pulses were separated by continuous 20 SCCM nitrogen gas purge with purge times that were longer for lower substrate temperatures to allow adequate purging of residual gases and quantified below.

Growth using ( t BuN)2(NMe2)2Mo and O3 was performed based on literature description. 6 The deposition temperature was set to 200 °C and ( t BuN)2(NMe2)2Mo temperature was set to 50 °C. ( t BuN)2(NMe2)2Mo was delivered to the chamber using a 0.10 s nitrogen boost, during which time the ALD precursor cylinder was pressurized with N2 (g) to approximately 20 psi (gauge), followed by a 2 s wait and 0.75 s pulse into the chamber. This boost, wait, and pulse procedure was repeated for 15 sequential ' microdoses ' to achieve saturation for a given Mo precursor exposure step. O3 was pulsed continuously through the orifice for 48 s. A purge time of 20 s between Mo and O3 half reactions was used. For all ALD growths on silicon, depositions were performed on 0.5 × 0.5-in. test-grade p-type 525 μ m thick Si(100) coupons. Coupons were ultrasonically cleaned for 5 min in isopropyl alcohol, rinsed in 18.2 M Ω water, and treated with UV ozone for 5 min prior to loading into the chamber.

For an attempt to grow MoOx films using Mo(CO)6, precursor pulse times were determined based on the literature. 13 The Mo precursor and O3 were pulsed for 4 and 40 s, respectively. The Mo precursor was held at 70 °C and deposition temperature was determined by varying the chamber temperature from 120 to 170 °C. No ALD growth was measured for any deposition using Mo(CO)6 as a Mo precursor.

Saturation studies for Mo( i PrCp)2H2 were performed at a chamber temperature of 150 °C with the Mo precursor held at 80 °C. As dosage saturation behavior was investigated for both co-reactants, the remaining parameters, including precursor dose times [Mo( i PrCp)2H2: 0.25 s and O3: 40 s], and the number of precursor pulses [Mo( i PrCp)2H2: 6 pulses and O3: 1 pulse] were kept constant. The N2 boost mode was also used for the Mo( i PrCp)2H2 precursor. In a separate experiment, deposition temperature was varied from 100 to 300 °C in increments of 25 °C to determine the upper and lower limits of the ALD growth temperature window. underwent an anneal at 350 °C for 20 min in air. MoOx film thickness was measured using a J. A. Woollam VASE spectroscopic ellipsometer (SE) and data were fit with a Cauchy model. Extinction coefficient, refractive index, and thickness were found using SE. Thickness, density, and roughness were analyzed by x-ray reflectivity (XRR) using a Panalytical Empyrean x-ray diffractometer. 14 Crystallinity was measured for as-deposited films on Si deposited at 100, 150, and 200 °C and a 350 °C-annealed film on Si deposited at 200 °C using the Panalytical Empyrean x-ray diffractometer for x-ray diffraction (XRD), measuring 2 Θ symmetric scans. Film composition and stoichiometry were measured using a SPECS XPS instrument. A survey scan was done to identify significant peaks, followed by high resolution core-level scans on the Mo 3d, O 1s, C 1s, and Si 2p peaks when applicable. Films were sputtered for 360 s with 5 keV Ar + to remove ∼ 10 nm of material to provide an accurate measure of carbon content in the bulk of the film. Additionally, chemical species in the film were found using Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy. The FTIR spectra of the MoOx films were collected in transmission mode in a Perkin Elmer 100 spectrometer equipped with N2 gas purge. Double side polished float zone Si (100) wafers from Virginia Semiconductor with 2 × 6 cm 2 dimensions were used for FTIR. These chips were cleaned by sonication in IPA (isopropyl alcohol) and 18.2 M Ω water followed by 5 min UVO cleaning for 5 min prior to deposition. For background, the clean DSP wafer FTIR spectrum was collected prior to the deposition to isolate the signal from the MoOx film. The measurements were averaged over 250 scans with 4 cm -1 scan resolution. For composition comparison, MoOx films of similar thickness were grown using (NtBu)2(NMe2)2Mo ALD.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Based on experimental vapor pressure measurements 15 by Zorin et al. , heating the Mo( i PrCp)2H2 precursor to 80 °C was determined to be sufficient to provide adequate vapor pressure for ALD, while maintaining thermal stability. However, this condition alone did not result in uniform growth across the reactor chamber with standard vapor draw delivery. To increase the dosage of Mo( i PrCp)2H2, a 0.1 s boost of N2 gas was introduced to the Mo precursor dosing step. Adding this boost increased uniformity and increased the GPC near the outlet relative to delivery based on purely vapor draw. The average GPC also increased significantly from 0.3 to 1.0 Å/cycle when using one pulse of Mo precursor without boost and with boost, respectively. For all remaining discussion, boost mode was used at a precursor temperature of 80 °C.

Growth saturation was investigated for both O3 and Mo ( i PrCp)2H2 independently by varying the dose of one precursor and keeping the other precursor dose constant (Fig. 1). Mo saturation was tested at a deposition temperature of 150 °C using a 40 s O3 dose time. Mo( i PrCp)2H2 saturated at approximately six pulses, with a GPC of 1.5 Å/cycle. From four to eight Mo precursor microdoses, the SUP remained low (2.0% -4.2%) also indicating nearly ideal ALD behavior. Using these saturated Mo conditions (six Mo microdoses), O3 dose time was separately varied. Saturation in the GPC occurred in the range of 20 -40 s dose time with a GPC of 1.4 -1.6 Å/cycle.

Optical constants for ∼ 30 nm thick films were obtained from SE (Table II). Values for refractive index ( n ) and extinction coefficient ( k ) are reported at a wavelength of 605 nm. Vos et al. 16 found n = 2.099 when using Mo(NtBu)2(NMe2)2 and O2 plasma as reactants. The value of n of crystalline α -MoO3 is reported to be 2.139. 17 For the MoOx deposited in this study at 100, 150, and 200 °C, the index of refraction was 1.44, 1.42, and 1.38. For comparison, films grown in our laboratory using Mo(NtBu)2(NMe2)2 had a refractive index of 1.62. Vos et al. found k to be 0.0164. k in crystalline α -MoO3 is measured as 0.006 31. 18 Extinction coefficients were 0.000 31 and 0.0063 for deposition temperatures 100 and 150 °C, respectively. The 200 °C deposition was once again the outlier, as its extinction coefficient was raised to 0.15, significantly higher than the values for lower deposition temperatures. MoOx deposited with Mo(NtBu)2(NMe2)2 in this study exhibited an extinction coefficient of 0.073. Generally, the low n values as compared to crystalline samples indicate low density material and possible contamination with residual ligand species.

Density and roughness values were obtained using XRR (Table II). The mass densities ranged from 2.39 to 2.85 g/cm 3 and increased with increasing deposition temperature. These densities correspond to only 50% -60% of the density of α -MoO3 (4.78 g/cm 3 ). 19 The increase in density seen with the increase in deposition temperature is possibly due to the removal of organic contaminants within the film and greater adatom mobility. This trend in density disagrees with the trend in n value for the 200 °C sample. Refractive index is expected to increase monotonically with density, but instead has the opposite effect, as density seems to increase when n decreases at 200 °C. This discrepancy is possibly due to the significant presence of carbon found in XPS data ( vide infra ). Incorporation of carbon into the films can lead to low density values and changes to n . Films deposited with Mo (NtBu)2(NMe2)2 were found to have a density of 3.04 g/cm 3 . The larger density of films deposited using Mo(NtBu)2(NMe2)2 may be due to higher film purity. FTIR spectra of as-deposited MoOx films using Mo( i PrCp)2H2 and Mo(NtBu)2(NMe2)2 precursors are compared with the FTIR spectra after annealing (Fig. 6). As-deposited films using Mo ( i PrCp)2H2 contained peaks corresponding to unsaturated aromatic rings (3000 -3100 cm -1 ), 23,24 triple bonded carbon (3300 cm -1 ), 23,24 and asymmetric and symmetric COO- stretch (1650 -1540 and 1450 -1360 cm -1 , respectively). 25 The presence of CHx and COO- species in as-deposited Mo( i PrCp)2H2 films is in agreement with the C1s spectra observed in XPS. As-deposited Mo (NtBu)2(NMe2)2 films contained peaks corresponding to N -H amine stretch (3400 -3250 cm -1 ), 26 single bond C -H stretching (2850 -3300 cm -1 ), 23 N -H primary bend in amines and asymmetric COO- stretch (1580 -1650 cm -1 ), 25 and aliphatic C -N stretch (1020 -1250 cm -1 ). 26 For both precursor chemistries, peaks corresponding to O -H (3250 -3650 cm -1 ) 26 and CO2 (2350 cm -1 ) 27 -29 are observed. The CO2 peak is attributed to incomplete removal from the atmosphere after 15 min of N2 purge. ## IV. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS

Substoichiometric MoOx with significant C content was grown using atomic layer deposition in the temperature range of 100 -225 °C using Mo( i PrCp)2H2 and O3. GPC in the ALD window ranged from 1.3 to 1.7 Å/cycle. Films are amorphous as-deposited and crystallized to α -MoO3 after annealing at 350 °C in air.

ARTICLE

Compositional analysis revealed high carbon concentrations in as-deposited films that were slightly higher relative to the comparison amido Mo precursor, with these concentrations decreasing after annealing. The C concentration might be reduced by using a higher concentration of ozone or an oxygen plasma. In comparison to films deposited using Mo(NtBu)2(NMe2)2 and O3, films grown with Mo( i PrCp)2H2 have a higher GPC and lower density.
