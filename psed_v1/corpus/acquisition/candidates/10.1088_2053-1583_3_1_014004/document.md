## Abstract

Aprocess for the self-limited layer synthesis ( SLS ) of WSe2 on SiO2 substrates has been developed that provides systematic layer number controllability with micrometer-scale ( &gt; 90% ) andwafer-scale ( ∼ 8 cm ) uniformity suitable electronic and optoelectronic device applications. This was con /uniFB01 rmedby the fabrication and testing of a WSe2 back-gated /uniFB01 eld effect transistor ( FET ) using Pd ( 30 nm ) as the contact metal, which exhibited p-type behavior with an on / off ratio of ∼ 10 6 anda /uniFB01 eld-effect hole mobility of 2.2 cm 2 V -1 s -1 value, which was higher than has been reported for WSe2-based FETs produced by conventional chemical vapor deposition. On the basis of these results, it is proposed that the SLS method is universally applicable to a range of device applications.

## Introduction

Two-dimensional ( 2D ) transition metal dichalcogenides ( TMDCs ) have attracted considerable attention [ 1 -7 ] as a result of their remarkable electronic and optoelectronic properties, which include a unique indirect-to-direct bandgap transition with reducing layer numbers [ 8 ] , high carrier mobility [ 9 ] and strong spin-orbital coupling due to broken inversion symmetry [ 10 ] . These exotic properties make 2D TMDCs particularly well suited for use in electronic and optoelectronic devices like photo / gas sensors and /uniFB01 eld effect transistors ( FET ) [ 11 ] . Indeed, WSe2 is of particular interest as a p-type substitute for n-type MoS2, as p-FETs based on exfoliated single-crystal WSe2 have been demonstrated to have a high hole mobility ( ∼ few hundreds of cm 2 V -1 s -1 ) and on / off ratio ( ∼ 10 8 ) [ 12 ] . Their practical application, however, has so far been limited by a poor wafer-scale uniformity, crystallinity, and layer number controllability. Several studies have succeeded in preparing atomically thin WSe2 by exfoliation [ 13 ] , the selenization of metal oxide thin /uniFB01 lms [ 14 ] , or chemical vapor deposition ( CVD ) [ 15, 16 ] , but these still failed to achieve the necessary large area uniformity and layer controllability. Consequently, there is still a clear need for an improved method of synthesizing atomically thin WSe2 for practical device application.

The authors of this paper have previously reported the self-limiting layer synthesis ( SLS ) of 2D MoS2 by means of a process in which the layer number is determined by temperature rather than the number of process cycles [ 17 ] . This is made possible by the chemically inactive nature of 2D MoS2, and provides for excellent layer controllability ( from monoto tri-layers ) and large area uniformity ( over 90% ) . The present study therefore looks at the feasibility of applying this method to the synthesis of WSe2 using WCl6 and diethyl selenide ( DESe ) . By using this liquid-phase organic precursor in place of the gaseous H2S reactant used with MoS2, the self-limiting growth characteristics of WSe2 become purely dependent on temperature after a certain number of process cycles. The layer uniformity, reproducibility, and controllability made by possible by this are herein discussed along with the electrical characteristics of the synthesized WSe2 when used in a backgate FET.

## Methods

## SLSWSe2growth

A tube furnace reactor was used to synthesize WSe2 directly onto SiO2 ( 285 nm ) / Si substrates using WCl6 and diethyl selenide ( DESe ) as the precursor and reactant, respectively. A bubbler containing the precursor was heated to 85 ° C to ensure an adequate vapor pressure for the precursor molecules to be carried into the tube by a pure Ar ( 99.999% ) carrier gas. In addition, H2 gas is injected along with DESe during the reactant time for assisting reduction of WCl6 and removal of Cl as form of HCl. The SLS cycle consisted of four steps, each with the same with atomic layer deposition ( ALD ) procedure of: precursor exposure for 4 s, a 5 s Ar purge, 3 s DESe reactant exposure, anda /uniFB01 nal 5 s Ar purge.

## Transfer of WSe2

The WSe2 synthesized on the SiO2 substrates was spin coated with polymethyl methacrylate ( PMMA ) at 4000 rpm for 60 s. After curing the PMMA at 100 ° C for 15 min, the sample was immersed in a 10% HF solution to etch away the SiO2 layer. The sample was then washed with DI water and transferred to a new SiO2 / Si substrate. Finally, the PMMA was removed using acetone, and the sample was washed with isopropyl alcohol.

## Back-Gated /uniFB01 eld-effect transistor fabrication

AWSe2-based FET was fabricated from three layers of WSe2 synthesized on a SiO2 ( 300 nm ) / Si substrate through the evaporation of Pd ( 30 nm ) electrodes by conventional photolithography and reactive ion ( O2 plasma ) etching.

## Characterization of WSe2

The uniformity achieved by the SLS process was assessed by synthesizing three layers of WSe2 onto 2 /uniF0A0 × /uniF0A0 8 cm 2 SiO2 substrates; the substrate size being limited in this instance by the diameter ( ∼ 3 cm ) and length ( 10 cm hot zone ) of the tube furnace used. To assess the cm-scale uniformity, Raman spectra ( HORIBA, Lab Ram ARAMIS; 532 nm laser excitation wavelength ) were obtained from eight different positions along the length of the sample. The crystallinity and atomic arrangement of the WSe2 were also evaluated through high-resolution TEM ( HRTEM ) analysis ( FEI Titan G2 Cube 60-300, accelerating voltage /uniF0A0 = /uniF0A0 80 kV ) . Optical microscopy ( Nikon ECLIPSE LV100ND ) , x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy ( XPS: Thermo U.K., K-alpha radiation ) and a voltage / current meter ( Keithley 4200, Keithley Instruments ) were also used in characterizing the WSe2 produced by SLS.

This signal weakens and becomes negligible as the number of layers is increased, as this induces a transition from a direct to an indirect band gap. This is concordant with previous results regarding the dependence of the PL signal on the number of layers, and further con /uniFB01 rms the temperature dependent nature of growth by SLS. In the XPS spectra of the monolayer WSe2 synthesized at 600 ° C shown in /uniFB01 gure 2 ( b ) , we see that the W4f core level has peaks at 38.4, 35.1 and 32.9 eV that correspond to the W5p3 / 2 , W4f5 / 2 and W4f7 / 2 levels, respectively. The Cl2p core level exhibits no discernible peaks, indicating that the WSe2 contained no XPS-detectable Cl species. From this, the stoichiometric ratio of Se:W was calculated to be 2.

This self-limiting aspect of the SLS process is believed to be the result of the same mechanisms previously identi /uniFB01 ed in the SLS of MoS2, which can be summarized as follows. Firstly, the fact that the surface of a TMDC is inherently chemically inert forces synthesis to proceed through physical adsorption [ 23, 24 ] . Secondly, the surface potential depth of WCl6 precursor molecules decreases with an increasing number of WSe2 layers due to their screening effect on the electric /uniFB01 eld [ 25 ] . Consequently, once a certain number of WSe2 layers have been formed at any given growth temperature with suf /uniFB01 ciently high thermal energy, most of the WCl6 molecules that are physically adsorbed on the WSe2 basal plane can be easily desorbed due to the reduced potential depth [ 23, 24 ] . This creates the same self-limiting growth behavior seen in the synthesis of MoS2 using a gaseous reactant, and shows that the process can be just as easily used with a liquidphase organic reactant. It therefore stands to reason that this SLS process could be applied to the synthesis of a wide range of two-dimensional TMDCs, regardless of the chalcogen reactant needed. It is noteworthy that our SLS WSe2 mechanism is totally different from the previous reported self-limiting CVD MoS2, which is limited by loaded dose of the precursor.

The wafer-scale ( 2 /uniF0A0 × /uniF0A0 8 cm 2 ) uniformity achieved by SLS is demonstrated by the consistency in color of the WSe2 shown in /uniFB01 gure 3 ( b ) , with the Raman spectra in /uniFB01 gure 3 ( c ) also showing very little variation between the eight different points tested. The E g 1 2 and A1g peaks in /uniFB01 gure 3 ( c ) were /uniFB01 tted to obtain the ratio between the two for each point, which are plotted in /uniFB01 gure 3 ( d ) . This found that the intensity ratio was consistently low, with an average value of 3.6 and a standard deviation of 0.11.
