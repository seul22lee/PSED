Solving the ALD infill of a nanocrystal network

A 1-D diffusion–reaction solver for the precursor concentration C(z,t) and coverage fraction θ(z,t) inside a porous nanocrystal film, from user-defined initial conditions — and for the three process parameters that decide whether the film ends up dense: dose time, purge time, and cycle count.

Modelled after A. Cendejas, D. Moher and E. Thimsen, “Modeling atomic layer deposition process parameters to achieve dense nanocrystal-based nanocomposites”, J. Vac. Sci. Technol. A 39, 012406 (2021). 10.1116/6.0000588

The formulation below is therefore the standard Knudsen-diffusion + Langmuir-chemisorption treatment of ALD in a porous medium, not a transcription of the paper’s equations. The model

Two coupled fields on one spatial coordinate

Depth z runs from the exposed film surface to the impermeable substrate. Precursor diffuses down the pore network and is consumed irreversibly where it meets bare nanocrystal surface.

(1)ε ∂C/∂t  =  ∂/∂z ( De ∂C/∂z )  −  Sv Γ ∂θ/∂t

Gas balance per unit total film volume. The sink is the surface reaction: every molecule that sticks leaves the pore gas.

(2)∂θ/∂t  =  s(θ) (v̅/4) C / Γ  −  kdes θ
s(θ)  =  s0 (1 − θ)n

Langmuir surface balance. The kinetic flux to the wall is (v̅/4)C; a fraction s(θ) of it reacts. n = 1 is ideal Langmuir, n > 1 reproduces the soft saturation of sterically hindered ligands, and kdes = 0 is the usual irreversible-chemisorption assumption.

z = 0J  =  (Cgas(t) − C)  /  ( 1/km + Δz/2De )

z = LJ  =  0

External mass transfer in series with the first half-cell at the top; an impermeable substrate at the bottom. km = ∞ (the default) collapses the inlet to a Dirichlet condition. Cgas(t) is the dose/purge waveform — a constant, or any callable, so arbitrary pulse trains are just a function.

Pore geometry and how it evolves

The network is treated as randomly packed spheres of radius r at solid fraction φ = 1 − ε. Each completed cycle grows a shell of thickness GPC · min(θA,θB) on the internal surface, and porosity follows the exact volume balance dε = −Sv dt.

Sv = 3φ/r   ·   rp = 2ε/Sv   ·   DK = (2/3) rp v̅   ·   De = (ε/τ) DK

Knudsen transport — at a pore radius of 3.33 nm the mean free path is far larger than the pore, so molecules collide with walls, not each other. Tortuosity defaults to Bruggeman, τ = ε−1/2. Below a set closure porosity the pore network is treated as sealed: De and Sv go to zero there, so a prematurely sealed surface layer starves everything beneath it.

Profiles

What the solver returns

Process parameters

The three numbers the model exists to produce

Dose time
175 ms

To bring every depth of the 1 µm film to 99% coverage on the first cycle. A dose 5× shorter penetrates only 440 nm.

Purge time
5.15 µs

For residual pore gas to fall to 10−3 of the dose density. Purging is pure diffusion out, so it is fast — roughly the bare pore diffusion time.

Cycles to dense
12

For a 200 nm film at 50% porosity with saturating doses throughout. Each cycle adds a 1.10 Å shell to 3.00 × 108 m2 of internal surface per m3.

Failure mode
28%

Porosity left at the bottom when the dose is fixed at its first-cycle value. The top reaches 0.4% and seals; after that no dose, however long, reaches the interior.

Pass dose_target= to run_cycles() and the dose is re-sized every cycle instead — 8.59 ms on cycle 1 rising to 4.18 s on cycle 12.

The quasi-steady mode is not a convenience — at α/ε above 106, in the last cycles before pore closure, the transient form overflows any stiff integrator. Dropping the negligible accumulation term also makes the gas balance linear in C, so each step is one tridiagonal solve. Purges always stay transient: the residual gas is exactly what a purge calculation measures.

Using it

Initial conditions are yours to set

Both fields accept a scalar, an array over the grid, or a callable of depth — so a partially covered film, a pre-charged pore volume, or a measured profile all go straight in.

from twin.nc_infill_model import NCFilm, Precursor, NCInfillModel

model = NCInfillModel(
    film      = NCFilm(thickness=1e-6, nc_radius=5e-9, porosity=0.5),
    precursor = Precursor("TMA", M=72.09e-3, sticking=0.01, pressure=100.0),
    T=473.0, gpc=1.1e-10, n_cells=120)

# user-defined initial conditions: scalar, array, or f(z)
res = model.simulate_half_cycle(
    duration   = 0.2,
    theta_init = lambda z: 0.4*np.exp(-z/2e-7),
    c_init     = 0.0)

res.C          # (n_t, n_z) pore-gas density  [m^-3]
res.theta      # (n_t, n_z) coverage fraction [-]
res.pressure   # same, as partial pressure    [Pa]
res.penetration_depth(0.5)

Process-parameter queries

model.saturation_dose_time(target=0.99)   # -> (0.175, result)
model.purge_time(residual=1e-3)          # -> (5.15e-06, result)

# full infill, dose re-sized every cycle
out = model.run_cycles(60, t_dose=1e3, t_purge=1e-4, dose_target=0.99)
out.porosity     # (n_cycles, n_z) after each cycle
out.dose_time    # (n_cycles,) seconds needed
out.cycles_to_infill

Assumptions worth knowing

  • Transport is pure Knudsen diffusion. At higher pressure or larger pores, add the molecular term in series — channel_model.py already does this for lateral high-aspect-ratio features.
  • The sphere-packing closure Sv = 3φ/r ignores overlap between grown shells, so surface area is over-estimated late in infill. The volume balance is exact regardless, and rp → 0 throttles transport before the error matters.
  • Coverage is reset between half-reactions: chemisorbed precursor is assumed fully consumed by the coreactant.
  • Growth per cycle is treated as a constant, independent of curvature and of the underlying surface.
